TACTICS goal - rules (Phase A framing, written 2026-11-04)

User ask (verbatim):
"still not good enough to capture the essence, now you are only describing physical
movements, which is not bad, but need to infer further why it's done, what's next.
badminton is a feedback loop, you hit depending on the opponent's state. read 100
badminton books to understand it like a national team coach"

Plain goal: the video should explain each shot as a choice made from the opponent's
state (where she is, which way she is moving, how balanced she is), say what that shot
sets up next, and then show whether that happened.

DONE means all of these are true, checked by files, not by my say-so:

1. Books
   a. Target 100 book-length badminton works (body text >= 15,000 words each).
   b. Legal only: public domain, open access licence, or an official free download
      from the author or publisher. No pirate copies. Quotes kept to <= 25 words.
   c. On-topic: about the sport. The Badminton Magazine / Badminton Library series and
      place-name uses are out. A text counts only if sport words (shuttle, racket,
      smash, clear, drop, net, rally) appear often enough (filter in manifest).
   d. Counted by type (old public-domain book, coaching manual, federation manual,
      thesis, open book). If fewer than 100 exist legally, report the true count and
      why. Never pad with short articles.
   e. A tactics codebook: each rule is "opponent state -> my choice -> expected reply",
      backed by >= 2 independent sources with short quotes and page/section pointers.

2. Tactics engine (per hit, from real pose tracks; shuttle is NOT tracked)
   a. Records: hitter, contact time, both players' court positions, opponent movement
      direction and speed, distance from base, balance/reach at contact.
   b. Infers: landing zone (from where the receiver makes the next contact), shot family
      (from contact height, time to next contact, depth), intent (a codebook rule),
      expected reply set, then checks it against the real next hit.
   c. Checked against my own by-eye labels on the chosen rallies:
      hit detection precision and recall >= 0.8; landing-zone agreement >= 70%;
      reply-prediction hit rate reported next to a naive baseline (no target, honest).

3. Video
   a. >= 60% of narration sentences say WHY (reason from opponent state) or WHAT NEXT,
      each tied to something measured and shown on screen (mini-court with both
      players, arrows, open space, opponent movement).
   b. v7 is scored with the same rubric as the baseline.
   c. Old gates still pass: picture/skeleton sync, pacing, voice inside its window,
      no silent gaps, no repeated action.

4. Judge: a blind judge from a different model prefers the new script on why/what-next
   and finds no unsupported claim (for example, no claim of tracking the shuttle).

5. Final judge: the user's eyes and ears. Not claimed in advance.

Scope: the full China Open 2025 WS final reel (8:25, CC BY 3.0, mostly one main camera
with a few replay angles) - choose the best rallies for tactics, not just 30 s clips.
Rigor: high (published claims about sport; honesty of counts).
Out of scope: shuttle tracking model, 3D, medical claims, paid sources, uploading footage.
